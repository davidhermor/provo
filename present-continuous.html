📖 Grammar Explanation

Visual models showing when and how to build the Present Continuous tense.

When Do We Use the Present Continuous?

1. Right Now

Actions taking place at the exact second of speaking.

Past ● NOW Future
  • Listen! Someone is playing the guitar.
  • She is talking on the phone right now.

2. Temporary Situations

Happening around now, for a limited time period.

Past [ THIS PERIOD ] Future
  • I'm staying at my sister's house this week.
  • He is studying for his English exam this month.

3. Changing Situations

Developing trends, gradual shifts, or progress in motion.

Start ↗ DEVELOPING Goal
  • The weather is getting colder.
  • My English is improving fast.

4. Future Plans

Definite arrangements with a fixed date, time, or person.

Now Arranged ★ FIXED DATE
  • We are having dinner with Mark tonight at 8:00.
  • I'm flying to London next Friday.

Sentence Structure (Form)

Form Structure Examples
Affirmative (+) Subject + am / is / are + verb-ing I am working. / She is reading. / They are running.
Negative (-) Subject + am not / isn't / aren't + verb-ing I'm not sleeping. / He isn't watching TV. / We aren't cooking.
Question (?) Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing? Are you listening? / Is it raining?
Wh- Question Wh-word + am / is / are + subject + verb-ing? What are you doing? / Where is she going?

Short Answers:

Are you studying? → Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Is he working? → Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
Are they coming? → Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Spelling Rules for -ing: Word-Building Equations

Observe the letter changes highlighted in teal and red pills:

1. Most Verbs

Simply add -ing with no changes:

work + -ing = working
play + -ing = playing
2. Verbs Ending in -e

Drop the silent -e before adding -ing:

mak -e̶ + -ing = making
writ -e̶ + -ing = writing
3. Short CVC Verbs

Double the final consonant before -ing:

run +n + -ing = running
sit +t + -ing = sitting
4. Verbs Ending in -ie

Transform -ie into -y + -ing:

l -ie̶ + -y + -ing = lying
d -ie̶ + -y + -ing = dying

Watch Out: Action Verbs vs. Stative Verbs

🟢 Green Light: Physical Actions

Physical activities and processes that can be started and stopped naturally take -ing:

✓ "I am cooking dinner right now."
✓ "They are running in the stadium."
🔴 Red Light: Stative Verbs

Verbs of heart, mind, senses & possession are states, NOT continuous actions:

like love hate want need know understand believe remember have (possession)
✓ Correct: I want a coffee right now.
✗ Incorrect: I am wanting a coffee.
✓ Correct: She knows the answer.
✗ Incorrect: She is knowing the answer.

📚 Related Vocabulary

Common signal words and useful action verbs frequently paired with the Present Continuous.

Time Expressions (Signal Words)

Right now / At the moment "I'm writing an email at the moment."
Today / Tonight "We're having pasta tonight."
This week / This month / These days "She's working from home this week."
Currently / Lately "They're currently looking for a new flat."
Look! / Listen! / Be quiet! "Look! It's snowing!"

Category Verbs Example in Context

🎭 Speaking & Roleplay Activity

Scenario: The Busy Phone Call — Pair up and roleplay this real-life telephone exchange.

Student A (Caller)
The Friend Calling

1. Call your friend and greet them.

2. Ask what they are doing right now.

3. Invite them to grab a coffee in 30 minutes.

Conversation Starter:
"Hi! What are you doing right now? Are you free to grab a coffee in 30 minutes?"
Student B (Receiver)
The Busy Friend

1. Answer the phone and say you are very busy.

2. Invent 3 things you or people around you are doing right now.

3. Explain why you can't meet now, and suggest a definite plan for tomorrow using the Present Continuous.

Conversation Starter:
"I'd love to, but I'm really busy right now! I'm tidying up, my brother is fixing the car... What are you doing tomorrow? I'm meeting Sarah at 4:00, do you want to join us?"
