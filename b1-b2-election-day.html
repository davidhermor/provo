Stage 04 · Reading & Process

How an Election Day Actually Works Behind the Scenes

For most voters, casting a ballot takes less than ten minutes. Yet behind that brief visit to a local school or community center lies a carefully coordinated civic operation run largely by ordinary citizens.

Before polling stations open—typically at 8:00 or 9:00 a.m.—poll workers arrive early to check the sealed ballot boxes, organize the printed ballot papers, and verify the electoral roll. In several European countries, including Spain, polling station staff are selected at random from registered voters, while in others they are trained civic volunteers or municipal employees.

Throughout the day, voters present their ID, have their names ticked off the register, and step behind a privacy screen or into a polling booth before dropping their envelope into the box. Postal votes that arrived earlier are added only after the doors close. Once voting ends, every single ballot is unfolded and counted by hand in front of accredited observers, and the official tally is signed before being transmitted to the central database.

Stage 05 · Grammar Spotlight

Describing Official Rules & Procedures (B1 / B2)

Process Passive + Institutional Modals
1. PROCESS PASSIVE

be + past participle

Used when the step itself matters more than who performs it.

• Ballot boxes are sealed before voting begins.
• Every paper ballot is counted by hand at night.
2. OBLIGATION & RULES

have to / must / be required to

Formal and everyday ways to explain voter and staff duties.

• Voters have to show valid photo ID.
• Poll workers are required to stay until the tally is signed.
3. PERMISSION & OPTIONS

be allowed to / can apply for

Explaining alternatives like postal ballots or time off work.

• Citizens away from home can apply for a postal vote.
• Employees are allowed to take paid time off to vote.
