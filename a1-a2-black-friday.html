Stage 04 · Short Reading

Two Friends, Two Shopping Styles

Every November, Carla and her brother Leo look at the Black Friday sales, but they shop in very different ways.

Carla makes a small list two weeks before Black Friday. She checks the normal prices of a warm winter coat and running shoes. On Friday morning, she opens two websites at home with a cup of tea. The coat has a 30% discount, so it is cheaper than in October. She buys the coat, closes her laptop, and saves her money.

Leo does not make a list. He goes to the big shopping center after work. The shops are noisy and crowded, and there are long queues at the checkout. He sees bright red “50% OFF” signs everywhere. Leo buys three T-shirts, new headphones, and a backpack. Later at home, he checks his receipt and realizes he spent more money than Carla—and he already has two backpacks!

Stage 05 · Grammar Spotlight

Comparing Prices & Shops (Comparatives)

A1–A2 Essential Grammar
1. SHORT ADJECTIVES + -ER

cheap → cheaper than

Add -er to short adjectives (1 syllable) + than.

• Online shops are often cheaper than small stores.
• Friday is busier than Tuesday. (busy → busier)
2. LONG ADJECTIVES → MORE

expensive → more expensive

Use more + adjective + than for longer words.

• Leather boots are more expensive than trainers.
• Online shopping is more convenient than queuing.
3. IRREGULAR FORMS

good → better · bad → worse

Two super common irregular comparatives for shopping:

• A 40% discount is better than a 10% discount.
• Saturday afternoon crowds are worse than morning crowds.
