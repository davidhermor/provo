4 FREQUENCY ZONES
High Frequency
normally · generally · most days

"I generally wake up at 7:00 AM on school days."

Mid Frequency
occasionally · from time to time

"From time to time, I take a one-hour nap in the afternoon to recharge."

Low Frequency
hardly ever · rarely · seldom

"She hardly ever eats meat because she follows a vegetarian diet."

Specific Intervals
every other day · twice a week

"We have physical education every other day."

GOLDEN RULE #1

The Main Action

[Subject] + [ONE-WORD ADVERB] + [Main Verb]. One-word adverbs always step in front of the main action.

✓ Perfect: "I usually finish my homework before dinner."
✗ Incorrect: "I finish usually my homework before dinner."
GOLDEN RULE #2

The ‘To Be’ Wall

[Subject] + [Am/Is/Are] + [ONE-WORD ADVERB]. One-word adverbs must go after the verb to be.

✓ Perfect: "I am rarely late for my first class."
✗ Incorrect: "I rarely am late for my first class."
GOLDEN RULE #3

The Heavy Phrases

Longer phrases (two or more words) belong at the end, or at the very beginning for emphasis.

• End: "I play the ukulele twice a week."
• Beginning: "Every other day, I walk to school with my friends."
TRAP 1

The Continuous Illusion

✗ Error: "I am waking up at 6:30 every morning."

Present Continuous is only for right now. Routines and permanent situations demand the Present Simple.

✓ Fix: "I wake up at 6:30 every morning."

TRAP 2

The Dropped ‘S’

✗ Error: "My brother always leave his shoes..."

Under pressure, students drop the -s. He / She / It demands third-person conjugation.

✓ Fix: "My brother always leaves..."

TRAP 3

Double Negatives

✗ Error: "I don't hardly ever watch TV."

Words like hardly ever, rarely, and seldom are already negative. Never add don't!

✓ Fix: "I hardly ever watch TV."

FRAME 1 · SOLID HABIT

“As a rule, I generally [ACTION].”

Example: "As a rule, I generally pack my school bag the night before."

FRAME 2 · RARE EVENT

“I hardly ever have time to [ACTION], but from time to time I manage to...”

Example: "I hardly ever have time to cook, but from time to time I manage to make pancakes."

FRAME 3 · CONTRAST FORMULA

“During the week I normally [A], but on weekends I occasionally [B].”

Show the examiner complex contrast between school days and weekends.

Oxford Test of English Practice · The Late Start Study

A New Morning Routine

Most teenagers normally wake up very early for school, often feeling exhausted before they even leave the house. Recently, scientists have suggested that a teenager’s natural body clock is completely different from an adult’s. Because of this, some secondary schools are experimenting with changing their daily routines. Instead of starting classes at 8:00 a.m., they are allowing students to sleep in and arrive at 9:30 a.m.

One school introduced this new timetable last September. The headteacher expected that students would simply go to bed later, but the results show something different. Students generally still go to sleep at their usual time, meaning they get an extra hour of rest. Teachers report that teenagers are hardly ever late now, and they seem much more awake during the first lesson of the day.

Mark, a 15-year-old student, usually plays basketball after school. He admits he was worried about the new routine at first. “Because we start later, we also finish later,” he explains. “I thought I wouldn’t have enough time for sports practice in the afternoon. But actually, I have so much more energy now that I run faster and play better. I definitely don’t want to go back to the old schedule.”

Sarah, another student, loves the change for a different reason. Before the new timetable, her family was always in a rush in the mornings. “We hardly ever had time to sit down together,” she says. “Now, we occasionally have breakfast as a family before I leave for school. It’s a much calmer way to start the day, and I don’t feel stressed when I walk into the classroom.”
